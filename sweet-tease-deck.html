Sweet Tease

Written, directed, and photographed by Ion Peia.

London. Ten years together. One night to decide if that's enough.

On the night of their tenth anniversary, Rachel and Mark’s celebration takes a hard turn. Two people who thought they knew each other are about to find out how deeply they can hurt each other, and what can never be undone.

15 minutes · one house · one night

The story

It's their anniversary. Ten years, a house full of friends, a decade of soft jazz and good wine. When the guests leave, the warmth leaves with them. What's left is a kitchen full of dirty plates, a half-finished book, and a question neither of them has said out loud in two years: what is actually still "theirs"?

The memory

In the middle of the fight, the film cuts to a beach in Cornwall, years earlier, before the house, before the book, before the silence. Mark and Rachel, young and unguarded. It's here, playing in the sand, that Rachel first says she wants a child. The present only gets crueler once we've seen it.

The ending

Back in the present, Mark says the thing that can't be unsaid. Rachel goes upstairs alone. In the bathroom, she finds the test she's been avoiding. It turns positive. The film ends before she decides anything, leaving her alone with the test and a locked door.

One choice, alone…

What it's about

Whose house this is.
Whose dream the book is.
Whose body the question is.

Sweet Tease is about which parts of a shared life were never actually shared.

Mood & cinematography

The party is warm, all candlelight, wine, and other people's laughter. The kitchen turns colder as it empties. The bathroom, at the end, is the coldest room in the film: small, and finally hers alone.

Natural light, always

Daytime interiors are staged near windows, letting real light do the heavy lifting. Taking cues from Terrence Malick and cinematographer Emmanuel Lubezki, the key light drifts in from one side, soft and organic, gently wrapping the actors in a living glow.

24mm Prime

Every scene is carried on a single 24mm prime. It's wide enough to give the emotion room to breathe, and close enough to sit uncomfortably, beautifully close, offering a direct line to the inner life of this couple.
