How this key-system works

Fletcher's starting point was a practical problem: modern pigments gave painters far more range than the eye could sensibly carry on one palette at once, yet a permanently fixed set of colours was too narrow for every subject. His answer, borrowed loosely from musical key-signatures, was to build each palette from a key-note and a fixed sequence of unequal steps around the hue circle — rather than picking colours by eye.

Twelve hues sit evenly around the wheel. From any key-note, a triad is built using uneven spacing rather than an even split — this avoids landing on a direct complementary pair while still keeping real variety and one pair of hues in close, lively contrast. Beyond the key-note itself, the other five points — Dual, Triad, +4th, +5th and +6th — can each be switched on or off independently under Palette points, so you can work from just two colours up to the full six in whatever combination a scenario calls for.

The wheel's rings move from full intensity at the rim to neutral at the centre in twenty graded steps, echoing Fletcher's own idea that distance from the middle of the wheel stands for a colour's intensity — with the raw neutral shown as a solid disc at the very centre. The Value and Chroma sliders shift that whole wheel lighter/darker and more/less saturated, so you can tune a key to a particular light before reading off hex values.

The interval pattern, the twelve base pigments, and the grading strip are all defined near the top of this file's script, so you can adjust the system to your own version without touching the drawing logic.

About

Ion Peia was born in 1990 and raised in Severin, Romania. His early interest in imagery began with his grandfather's film photography and quilt pattern books, and throughout primary and secondary school he was always the one who could draw.

He turned his focus to drawing and painting around 2011, shortly after completing the Interior Design undergraduate program at the University of Arts and Design in Timișoara, Romania.

Ion has always been curious about how images imprint themselves on memory and become permanent, some so visceral they stay with us for a lifetime. His work centers on the study of the human, and on why certain faces, figures, and the feelings they evoke remain so vivid in our minds.

Ion currently lives in London, UK.
